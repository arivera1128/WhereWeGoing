| D-28 | **Accepted September 27, 2026.** Recommendation scoring version 1 gives deal strength 65% and restaurant/learned food fit 35%. Removed places are excluded. A 1-star place cannot be featured; a 2-star place can be featured only as a strong-deal fallback. Untried places receive neither a penalty nor a novelty bonus. A food trait is blocked from the featured result only after at least four ratings with at least 75% negative. Three alternatives include the next two acceptable scores and, when available, a different category. Consumer cards show deal terms, verification and an editable restaurant rating rather than algorithm explanations or deal-usefulness votes. A confirmed 1-star tap immediately reruns the current ranking. Ratings 2–5 save immediately but keep the current Tonight's Pick stable until a later recommendation session. Internal score components remain available for testing. | PRODUCT, ARCHITECTURE, USER_JOURNEY, Android client: establishes a testable first scoring policy while keeping the visible decision flow concise and preventing an unexpected mid-flow shuffle. This supersedes the visible explanation and usefulness portions of D-02, D-20 and D-23. Geography, distance, structured validity and broader behavior learning remain future work. |
| D-29 | **Accepted September 27, 2026.** The app still recommends a restaurant when no active deal exists because choosing where to eat is the primary value. A future offer never boosts tonight's score or appears as tonight's deal. Home shows only the nearest eligible upcoming offer within seven days. Active uncertain offers are labeled “Possible deal” with last-checked context and a prompt to confirm with the location; they receive less score weight than verified offers. A no-deal plan and its later check-in use restaurant language rather than claiming deal use. | PRODUCT, USER_JOURNEY, recommendation engine and Android UI: separates restaurant choice, current deal confidence and future planning while giving users a reason to return. |
| D-30 | **Accepted September 27, 2026.** Tester controls live in a visually separated **Prototype tools** menu destination and are available only when Android marks the build debuggable. Version 1 shows current test state, simulates the day without changing the device clock, exposes the existing non-destructive new-user Food Profile preview, and provides a confirmed full local reset. An active day simulation is labeled throughout the app. | Android client, USER_JOURNEY and validation workflow: makes important states repeatable without exposing test controls in a release build or silently changing saved data. |
| D-31 | **Accepted September 27, 2026.** A completed Home check-in replaces its card with a positive inline acknowledgement for about four seconds. The dashboard updates immediately, and no popup, new screen or acknowledgement tap is added. Worked, failed and unused outcomes receive distinct copy. | Android client and USER_JOURNEY: make the result visible and encouraging while keeping check-in a single-action flow. |
| D-32 | **Accepted September 28, 2026.** The approximately 30-second aspiration is measured with returning external testers and representative restaurant/deal data, from Home becoming visible through confirmation of a dinner plan. Record completion time, help required and recommendation-versus-alternative choice; measure onboarding separately. Owner walkthroughs may identify friction but do not validate the target. Set the minimum sample and acceptable completion rate after an initial pilot. | PRODUCT, ROADMAP and validation: defines a credible measurement protocol without claiming that hard-coded supply and one product-owner walkthrough are real-user evidence. |
| D-33 | **Accepted September 28, 2026.** A single-location independent restaurant still uses separate Restaurant and Location records. The restaurant holds the continuing business identity; its physical outlet holds address-specific facts. Opening another outlet adds a Location, while a move retires the prior Location and creates another so historical references remain accurate. | DATA_MODEL and future persistence: validates the simplest restaurant scenario without introducing a special one-location representation. |
| D-34 | **Accepted September 28, 2026.** A chain deal can be INCLUDED, EXCLUDED or UNKNOWN per location. Included locations may show a confirmed offer, excluded locations do not show it, and unknown locations may show a **Possible deal** with **Confirm with this location**. User worked/did-not-work results become evidence for that location and time and may adjust confidence as evidence accumulates; one report does not establish chain-wide truth. | DATA_MODEL, DEAL_LIFECYCLE and recommendation UI: keeps uncertain offers useful while preserving honest labels and location-level evidence. Numeric weighting, conflict thresholds and decay remain open. |
| D-35 | **Accepted September 28, 2026.** Offer timing uses the restaurant location's local time. Same-day offers may appear before their opening time for planning; overnight windows remain active after midnight through their stated end; expired offers are ineligible but retain history. An active deal must have at least 25 minutes remaining to appear as an actionable tonight option, using the same cutoff for every household type. | PRODUCT, DATA_MODEL, DEAL_LIFECYCLE and recommendation filtering: avoids wasting result space on deals that are about to end while letting users judge whether a usable remaining window works for them. |
| D-36 | **Accepted September 28, 2026.** Child-age deals require at least one known qualifying child and are hidden for households with no qualifying child or insufficient age data. Membership deals remain visible with a clear requirement because a user may choose to enroll. Verified enrollment guidance may describe app, website or in-location signup and link to the official destination; store it separately from eligibility with its source and last-checked time. | PRODUCT and DATA_MODEL: applies hard filters to known age mismatches while preserving actionable membership opportunities without overstating signup availability. |
| D-37 | **Accepted September 28, 2026.** The documented logical model is accepted after review of independent restaurant, mixed chain participation, recurring/overnight/expired timing and age/membership eligibility scenarios. | DATA_MODEL, ROADMAP and architecture: access-pattern and physical-design work may use these boundaries. This is not a database selection or a final physical schema. |
| D-38 | **Accepted September 30, 2026.** Use a normalized relational schema as the working reference model without treating that as the final database selection. Major records use application-owned UUID primary keys. External provider IDs are separate mappings, and `location.restaurant_id` is a required foreign key to `restaurant.restaurant_id`. | RELATIONAL_MODEL, DATA_MODEL and backend review: makes fields, relationships and constraints concrete while keeping provider identity replaceable and the final storage choice open. |
| D-39 | **Accepted September 30, 2026.** Restaurant classifications use a typed `restaurant_attribute` table and a many-to-many assignment table. Initial types are cuisine, dining style and food trait; `(attribute_type, name)` and `(restaurant_id, attribute_id)` are unique. | RELATIONAL_MODEL and recommendation inputs: supports multiple classifications and new values without adding restaurant columns or storing comma-separated lists. | D-40 | **Accepted September 30, 2026.** A `deal` is the stable offer identity and its consumer terms live in `deal_version`. Drafts may be edited, but published versions are immutable; corrections or changed terms create a new version. Recommendation and outcome events reference the exact version shown. Supersession, withdrawal and parent-deal retirement preserve prior terms and history. | RELATIONAL_MODEL, event design and lifecycle implementation: prevents current edits from changing the meaning of historical recommendations or user reports. |
| D-41 | **Accepted September 30, 2026.** Recurring schedules use one `deal_schedule` row per weekday/time window rather than a day bitmask, array, or JSON field. Rows reference `deal_version`, use the location's local weekday/time, explicitly represent all-day and overnight windows, and become immutable when their version is published. | RELATIONAL_MODEL and validity queries: prioritizes readable SQL, enforceable constraints and straightforward indexing; the modest additional row count is acceptable. |
| D-42 | **Accepted September 30, 2026.** Published deal terms remain immutable while location participation is stored as effective-dated `deal_location_applicability` assertions for a specific deal version and location. A change closes the prior row and inserts another; at most one row is current per version/location. Confidence is a derived summary backed by later evidence records, not a replacement for them. | RELATIONAL_MODEL, lifecycle and recommendation history: location knowledge can change without manufacturing a new terms version, while past recommendations remain explainable. |
| D-43 | **Accepted September 30, 2026.** Deal conditions are separated into audience requirements, purchase requirements, redemption methods, usage limits and exclusion/combination rules; location and time retain their dedicated models. The engine may contextually collect editable self-reported user/household eligibility characteristics when a relevant nearby deal makes them useful. Eligible, not eligible and unknown remain distinct, and self-reporting is not documentary verification. | RELATIONAL_MODEL, DATA_MODEL, PRODUCT and recommendation inputs: supports real restaurant terms beyond age and membership without bloating onboarding or confusing who qualifies with how an offer is redeemed. Exact typed condition tables follow this accepted framework. |
| D-44 | **Accepted September 30, 2026.** Contextual eligibility answers distinguish `SELF`, `HOUSEHOLD_MEMBER`, `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, and `UNKNOWN`. The app stores a user-confirmed matching attribute, not proof or formal verification, and may retain the deal version that prompted collection. No other household member's identity is required. | RELATIONAL_MODEL, DATA_MODEL, PRODUCT and recommendation engine: improves matching while preserving who may qualify, why the question was asked and the restaurant's authority to verify redemption. |
| D-45 | **Accepted September 30, 2026.** Expandable eligibility characteristics use definition/response rows rather than one nullable user column per characteristic. A missing response to a structured deal requirement is the engine's unknown state and can trigger the definition's contextual prompt. Birthday uses recurring month/day occasion data; birth year is not collected solely for birthday offers. Structured conditions coexist with immutable free-text terms, disclaimers and official links. | RELATIONAL_MODEL, DATA_MODEL and recommendation engine: keeps new characteristics queryable without schema growth, supports calendar-based matching and retains details that cannot yet be evaluated automatically. |
| D-46 | **Accepted September 30, 2026.** Deal requirements use explicit condition groups. Every group on a version must pass; within a group, `ANY` means OR and `ALL` means AND. Conditions use a shared base record plus exactly one typed detail record, and the complete structure becomes immutable with the published deal version. Arbitrary nested rule expressions are deferred. | RELATIONAL_MODEL and recommendation engine: supports common combinations such as `(veteran OR first responder) AND dine-in AND qualifying purchase` with inspectable SQL and without building a general rules language. |
| D-47 | **Accepted October 1, 2026.** A typed `deal_attribute_requirement` connects one base condition to an eligibility attribute and declares `SELF_ONLY` or `HOUSEHOLD_ALLOWED`. `SELF` satisfies either; `HOUSEHOLD_MEMBER` satisfies only household-allowed offers; not eligible fails and missing/unknown may prompt. | RELATIONAL_MODEL and recommendation engine: makes audience matching executable without adding user columns or storing documentary verification. |
| D-48 | **Accepted October 1, 2026.** Purchase requirements are saved as immutable, classified deal conditions with consumer and source wording. | D-49 | **Accepted October 1, 2026.** Redemption channels use a controlled lookup and an immutable many-to-many bridge to `deal_version`, with optional channel-specific instructions. | D-50 | **Accepted October 1, 2026.** Usage limits remain immutable, classified deal-condition text for the current scope. Structured redemption counters, quantities and reset periods are deferred until an accepted engine rule, reviewed query, or merchant integration can use dependable redemption facts. Self-reported deal outcomes remain separate and are not treated as verified transaction accounting. | RELATIONAL_MODEL, DATA_MODEL and recommendation engine: retains accurate terms without building a redemption ledger the product cannot currently support. |
| D-51 | **Accepted October 1, 2026.** Exclusions and combination rules remain immutable, classified deal-condition text unless another accepted structure already represents the restriction. Do not build a stacking rules system while the app recommends one offer at a time and lacks checkout integration; revisit it when multiple simultaneous offers or checkout require machine evaluation. | RELATIONAL_MODEL, DATA_MODEL and recommendation engine: preserves accurate restrictions without duplicating redemption-channel data or speculatively implementing coupon calculation. |
| D-52 | **Accepted October 1, 2026.** `app_user` is a stable application identity independent from login providers. A guest installation links to an application user; future signup adds an `auth_identity` to that same user so profile and history survive migration. Household access uses a user/household bridge, allowing one-user MVP behavior and future shared accounts without making email or provider IDs primary keys. | RELATIONAL_MODEL, DATA_MODEL and future authentication: establishes an anonymous-to-account migration path while deferring provider, credential, recovery and sharing implementation. |
| D-53 | **Accepted October 1, 2026.** Each child uses an anonymous `household_child` row that supports either optional full `birth_date` or entered age plus `age_as_of_date`, without conflicting active sources. | D-54 | **Accepted October 1, 2026.** Restaurant ratings and exclusions belong to individual `app_user` records. `RATED`, `NOT_TRIED`, and no response remain distinct; a one-star rating is not a permanent exclusion. Exclusions are effective-dated separately. Future household linking or dining groups may combine independent profiles through join/context records without merging them, but collaboration workflows and group-scoring rules are deferred. | RELATIONAL_MODEL, DATA_MODEL and recommendation engine: keeps the main user's food profile authoritative today and leaves a non-destructive path to family or friend recommendations later. |
| D-55 | **Accepted October 1, 2026.** Explicit restaurant-attribute preferences and learned affinities are stored separately. Explicit preferences are user-authored and are never overwritten by learning. Learned affinity is a rebuildable projection carrying algorithm version, score, evidence count and calculation time; ratings/events remain source evidence. Dietary restrictions and allergies are outside ordinary taste learning and must not be inferred. | RELATIONAL_MODEL, DATA_MODEL and recommendation engine: preserves user intent, supports explainable recalculation and prevents derived taste signals from masquerading as safety requirements. |
| D-56 | **Accepted October 1, 2026.** Explicit dietary patterns such as vegetarian or vegan may later be modeled as separate hard requirements, never inferred preferences. ## Accepted MVP additions — October 2, 2026

- **D-61 — Manual submission review:** signed-in users submit a short text offer tied to an existing restaurant/location or a missing-place entry. Reviewer resolves identity, duplicates, schedule, terms and eligibility; unclear material facts cause a hold. Only approved entries become visible as possible deals, without equating approval with restaurant verification. My submissions includes status, notes and held-entry update/resubmission. Rationale: low entry friction and a testable manual publication boundary. PRODUCT, ROADMAP and tracker updated; implementation remains pending.
- **D-62 — Guest/account permissions:** guests retain recommendation, plan and check-in access, including worked/did-not-work outcomes. Only signed-in users submit deals or report problems. Optional sign-in from menu/submission preserves guest profile/history. Provider, recovery and cross-device semantics remain open. Refines D-52 and Q-07; authentication implementation is deferred until shared submissions.
- **D-63 — Problem reports:** signed-in users choose offer ended, incorrect details, wrong location or other, optionally adding a note. Route to review without automatically editing/withdrawing an offer. Keep reports distinct from meal outcomes. Rationale: catalog corrections need review and accountable contributions.
- **D-64 — Maps handoff:** include Open in maps on location details, with Copy address fallback when no maps app can open it; available to guests. Does not record intent or a visit. Supersedes the native-navigation exclusion in the earlier tester-ready scope only for this small external handoff; in-app maps and GPS tracking remain deferred.
- **D-65 — Deferred launch features:** favorites, photo/menu uploads, automated approval/trusted-contributor shortcuts, GPS tracking and push notifications are outside MVP. Revisit approval automation after manual workflow tests provide evidence. This limits launch scope without removing future design seams.

These decisions are accepted product requirements, not completed application features. Shared storage/authentication, detailed screen behavior and operational implementation remain necessary.

- **D-66 — Offline scope:** offline mode is post-launch. Shared MVP recommendations/community features may require internet, with clear connection messaging. This does not remove existing local Room persistence. Rationale: focus launch work on the connected core loop.
- **D-67 — Failure and retirement:** one failed-deal check-in flags review, not automatic hiding or restaurant dislike. Reviewer-confirmed ended offers leave current recommendations but preserve original historical evidence. Reliability aggregation remains open.
- **D-68 — Existing plan availability:** preserve the user's chosen place when an offer ends today or is withdrawn; distinguish the messages, offer Choose another place and retain earlier-use check-in. The 25-minute cutoff gates new recommendations only. Rationale: avoid silently changing user intent or blocking historical reporting.
- **D-69 — Outcome correction:** include Edit outcome in meal history, brief Undo after No and Edit last check-in on Home until the next plan is selected. Preserve access to the original expired offer for correction. No adds no meal/count; corrected Yes creates a meal. Corrections update totals/reliability and preserve prior-response provenance. No Recent plans section. Rationale: recover accidental answers with minimal visible history. Detailed correction storage and signal-reversal mechanics remain to implement.

PRODUCT, ROADMAP and PROJECT_STATUS carry D-66–D-69; these are accepted but unimplemented requirements.

## October 2, 2026 — accepted development direction

- **D-70 — Supabase/PostgreSQL proof:** user accepted the suggested bounded development experiment following comparison with Firebase. SQL experience, relational design and import/review workflows favor PostgreSQL. Prepare a Free development project, location import, published-only guest reads and reviewer-restricted publication. No paid deployment or complete production-schema approval is implied. Local Room remains in use. ARCHITECTURE, tracker and backend/proof describe limits and verification.
- **D-71 — Reviewer interface:** the product owner is the sole MVP reviewer; use a small browser-based review page with backend-enforced permissions. Additional reviewer assignments and a full operations portal are later scope. A development login/publication proof is not the full review workflow.
- **D-72 — Location import direction:** CSV/flat-file ingestion is the preferred starting approach for the initial market; preserve application UUIDs and separate import references. Duplicate/matching validation and source rights remain required. Fixture imports use prototype addresses explicitly not reverified; no restaurant-data provider or current production dataset is approved.

## D-73 — Shared catalog refresh and identity preservation

- **Date:** October 3, 2026
- **Status:** Accepted; bounded development implementation completed.
- **Decision:** Supabase owns the shared catalog; Room holds an atomic validated snapshot and personal data. Refresh at opening/eligible foreground returns; freeze during dinner selection. Preserve local identities, ratings, exclusions, history and the original plan. Retire withdrawn rows instead of deleting historical references. Failure pauses new/replacement recommendations, offers Retry and leaves check-in/cancellation available with an offer-status warning.
- **Rationale:** Avoid partial updates, shuffled choices and loss of saved preferences. Offline recommendation support remains deferred.
- **Consequences:** Schema 6 maps shared UUIDs onto existing local keys. Current DEV endpoint includes three locations, excludes all synthetic offers and therefore supplies restaurant-only picks. Reviewed real offers and production setup remain pending.
- **Affected:** PRODUCT, ARCHITECTURE, ROADMAP, PROJECT_STATUS, Android repositories and backend/proof/004_app_catalog.sql.

## D-76 — Editable manual review and test isolation

- **Date:** October 4, 2026
- **Status:** Accepted; development implementation prepared.
- **Decision:** Reviewer can edit a separate offer draft, match or explicitly create a restaurant/location, save/clarify/reject/approve, and identify test submissions. Preserve the original submission. Test entries and test-created places never reach recommendations.
